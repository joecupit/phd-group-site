Achieving product quality control beyond traditional set-point control strategies remains a constant challenge within chemical, pharmaceutical, formulations and other industries. These sectors face various uncertainties that complicate control efforts, leading to large reductions in efficiency, and potential product and raw material wastage. For this reason, operational space control methods have become increasingly popular, as they offer greater process flexibility compared to rigid set-point controls and allow the system to operate with minimal concern of product quality violations. This study presents a novel framework that systematically identifies flexible and optimal operational regions by integrating dynamic optimisation, machine learning surrogate modelling, and unsupervised clustering. The framework was applied to a real-world batch formulations process, for which it was shown to generate high-quality flexible regions, robust to industrial uncertainties. In a direct comparison to a recent literature approach, the proposed methodology demonstrated reduced computational expense and larger operational regions, enhancing process flexibility for complex manufacturing systems. Overall, the framework showcased great potential for developing flexible and optimal process flow operation diagrams for the product quality control of systems with large uncertainties by improving process economics and sustainability whilst simplifying control efforts.
